# Accord — Mike's rulings, in date order

Each line is a ruling Mike made, with its date and where it is recorded. A ruling is not reopened by an AI; if you think one is wrong, say so in your report with the reason and leave the behaviour as ruled. Rulings on the predecessor standard (MDP) that Accord inherits are in `~/Projects/SOMA/shared-cognition/mdp-agreed-model.md`; the ones that still bind are repeated here.

## Inherited from MDP (2026-09-01 to 09-12)

- **Assent is bracketed, not silent** (2026-09-03). An explicit mark on line A and an explicit mark on a later line B imply at least "seen" on every line between them, revisions included. Lines after the reader's last mark are unread, not agreed.
- **A ruling is never inferred from an agree mark** (protocol R4). A decision has its own control.
- **The ringer list** (2026-09-12, Q4 ratified): a round closes with a few writer-planted or writer-flagged sentences the reader should not have agreed with, placed where attention can reach them. Changes the reader scrolled past show as change marks in the text, not as a list.
- **Answer format** (2026-09-12): "1. Conclusion or recommendation. 2. Reason (if needed) 3. Yes/No etc." Clicking No starts an inline edit "No, because " with the cursor after "because".
- **Never ship a ratification surface as a bare `.md` file** (2026-09-03): "Markdown document is not MDP and so I cannot respond there." Define every term the page uses.
- **The Waiting on Mike page renders no change marks and no ringer list** (2026-09-12): "I don't need to know how you changed it, only that it is waiting." Jointly edited documents keep the marks.

## The Accord rules (2026-09-24)

- **Mike, 2026-09-24, in the Claude Code session "Accord Editor usability discussion": "We are in accord on the document."** The document is the Accord "The Accord rules", https://proof.vpsmikewolf.duckdns.org/d/gfmd0z5p. It holds Mike's thirteen rules plus eight proposals from Claude, which Mike accepted on the page. Both parties confirmed it. It is the latest statement of how an Accord works, so where it and an earlier line in this file disagree, it wins.
- The model in one line: nobody marks lines. A passage is in accord because one party wrote it and the other accepted it, or because it is a kind of change accepted in advance (the AI's definitions, spelling and punctuation fixes, and clarifying rewordings). An open issue is a change one party made that the other has not yet accepted, and it is the only kind of open item.
- Superseded by it, with the old words kept above:
  - 09-03 "Assent is bracketed" → nothing is inferred from reading any more.
  - 09-19 bundles, "accepting is not agreement" (`BUNDLE_POLICY.acceptIsAgreement = false`) → under rule 6, accepting a change is agreement.
  - 09-23 "the document is always shown whole, with the Review list beside it" → from a reader's next visit on, the reader sees only open issues, with a way to get context and a way to show the whole Accord. Nothing folds or moves while the reader is reading; that part of 09-23 stands.
  - Seen, Agreed and Approved marks as work a reader does (the Margin's Line tab, the A and R keys, "Agree with this section") → retired. The editor may still record, silently, what a reader has read.
  - The twelve Open kinds in `src/shared/open-view.ts` → one kind, the open issue.
- Closed by it: the open question on unread lines. `OPEN_VIEW_POLICY.unreadLinesAreOpen` stays false, because under rule 12 a line nobody changed is not an open issue.
- Also agreed in it: changes accepted in advance take one line at the end of the Accord, with a control that shows them in the text; a change that alters the meaning is never accepted in advance; rejecting a proposal leaves the original standing until the proposer accepts it or proposes again; while a change is open the reader sees the original struck through and the new words inserted, with the reason beside them, and once it is agreed only the agreed words show; the AI never asks a question without proposing an answer; each term has one definition across all Accords, held by one consolidating Accord; a later change reopens a confirmed Accord, which is then confirmed again. The rules hold in every Accord and in both directions, except clerical work, which stays with the AI. They are written for two parties; a rule for Accords with more people is still to come.
- Kept unchanged: a thread and a proposal are one object; no review pop-ups; Option+click edits a link's words; "?" means clarify; undo for every change; the direct-editing gate stays off.
- **Layout suggestion, same session (not yet a ruling):** "The open items currently in the left panel is useful. The right hand panel (agree, suggest change, etc) is not. You might consider moving the right panel to the left and restoring the list of accords to the left with an option for closing the panel and having more space."

## Accords that act (2026-09-25)

- **Mike, 2026-09-25, in the session that builds the editor, accepted all ten proposals and the build order of the Accord "Accords that act", https://proof.vpsmikewolf.duckdns.org/d/wa8dhyv7.** It came from his message: "The accord editor has a couple of uses. 1. One is agreeing on a fundamental idea as a collective of smaller ideas. 2. Acting on an idea may have tasks associated with it. The "Waiting for Mike" accord is almost entirely a set of actions. 3. If a task is accepted it can be delegated to the AI team. ... 4. "Seven decisions in the editor design" does not give me a way to act. Instead it should point me to the Accord and it is marked done automatically when I have made all the needed decisions, and also whatever conversation was asking for this should be informed so it can be continued. So the Waiting on Mike should be reviewed and rewritten. Also the ability to reorder the list could be carried out by me dragging and dropping items in the right hand Review list, or presenting an outline list". What he accepted:
  - An Accord holds idea items, which are agreed on, and action items: tasks with an owner, a rule for when they are done, and the conversation that asked for them.
  - Accepting an action item hands it to the team. The team comes back to Mike only for what it cannot or will not do, through one control on the item, never a set of instructions. "Cannot" means his body or identity (a fingerprint or passkey, his own sign-in, a conversation with a person); "will not" is a short published list (moving money, typing his passwords or card numbers, speaking for him to someone new).
  - A decision item points to the Accord holding the decisions, counts those still needing Mike, and closes itself at zero. When any item is done, the conversation that asked is told.
  - Mike reorders by dragging in the right-hand Review list; in an action Accord the order is the priority. A move is a proposed change like any other (point 8 of "The Accord editor"), but in Waiting on Mike his own moves apply at once. View offers an outline with the same dragging.
  - Build order: 1, the rewrite of Waiting on Mike, which needs no new code (proposed the same day as the Accord "Waiting on Mike, rewritten", https://proof.vpsmikewolf.duckdns.org/d/ma32fupv); 2, decision items that count, open and close themselves and tell the conversation that asked; 3, action items handed to the team; 4, dragging in the Review list and the outline (step 6 of the editor's build order).
- Until step 2 exists, the session that publishes an Accord watches it itself (a poll of the server's state for its proposals), because Mike asked: "all items accepted. Did I need to tell you that?"
- **Mike, same day: "I can navigate using J/K but I can't accept without moving my mouse to the sidebar and clicking. I'd often like to accept and then make a change."** Point 4 of "The Accord editor" ("Typing A accepts the item selected in the right-hand list") now reads: A and Delete act on the open item J and K reached, whether the Review list or the document holds the keyboard, and the focus stays on it; S then edits that item as a live proposal. Enter in the document still does not start writing, because Mike's 2026-09-21 list asked for S to be the edit key and Enter to leave a selected passage alone (`scripts/mike-0921-check.mjs` item 2). This supersedes the 2026-09-24 reading "A belongs only to Review" (`REVIEW_KEYS_POLICY`: "commands belong to the focused list").
- **Mike, same day, in the chat of the Accord "Colleague or Tool" (u948lq35), the first two messages he sent to the team from an Accord:** "The Share dialog does not let you set privileges associated with the link. The "View Activity" button is not a privilege level but goes to the activity screen" and "Terms should be at the top of accords. Hovering gives a "?" and a very small tooltip. Clicking gives the tooltip that I would like hover on desktop to provide". So:
  - The link's setting (only invited people, anyone can read, comment and chat, anyone can edit) was there for Owners, but Mike had stopped being one. Owner rights come from the SOMA session's admin check, which lapses after a day unless the browser renews it, and only the library pages loaded the script that renews it. Document pages now renew it too, and the page re-reads its rights when the renewal lands.
  - View activity leaves the Share dialog (it stays in File › View activity), so nothing under the link setting reads as a privilege level.
  - An Accord's Terms section goes at the top. On a desktop, resting the pointer on a linked term shows the definition box a click shows, and the browser's own small tooltip is gone.
  - Nothing answered the chat: an AI answering there is step 5 of the editor's build order. Until it is built, the session that builds the editor reads the chats of the Accords it watches every minute.
